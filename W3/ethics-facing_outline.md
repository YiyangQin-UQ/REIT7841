# Test Plan Outline for Ethics Preparation

## Study Title
Non-technical collaborative Tangram study comparing natural view and shared external camera view

## Purpose
This study explores how an externally mediated shared view affects collaboration, communication, and workspace awareness in a two-person Tangram task. The findings will inform the design of a later VR prototype.

## Participants
- Adult participants
- 2 participants per session (1 pair)
- Approximately 2–6 pairs
- Voluntary participation

## Study Format
A non-technical, formative user study using a physical Tangram task.

Two conditions:
- **Condition A:** natural direct view of the tabletop
- **Condition B:** shared external camera view on a screen; no direct tabletop viewing allowed

Within-subjects design with counterbalanced order.

## Procedure
Each pair will:
1. read the study information and provide consent
2. complete a short warm-up task
3. complete the Tangram task in both conditions
4. complete short post-task ratings and brief verbal feedback

Estimated total session time: 15–20 minutes.

## What the Study Is Looking For
- whether participants can complete the task using only the external camera view
- how communication and coordination change across conditions
- whether workspace awareness becomes more difficult under screen-mediated viewing
- what design issues should be considered in a later VR prototype

## Data Collected
- completion status
- completion time
- researcher observation notes
- short subjective ratings
- optional audio/video recording, if approved

## Risks
This is a low-risk study. Possible minor discomfort may include frustration or fatigue during the puzzle task. Participants may pause or withdraw at any time.

## Consent and Privacy
Participants will receive an information sheet and provide informed consent before taking part. Data will be de-identified where possible and stored securely for research purposes only.

## Expected Value
This study will help refine the later VR study design, including viewpoint rules, task structure, and suitable behavioural measures.
